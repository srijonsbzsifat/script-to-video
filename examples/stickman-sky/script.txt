Look up on a clear day. The sky is blue. But why is it not green, or purple?

{pop:sun.svg @right}Sunlight looks white, but it is really every colour of the rainbow mixed together.

{pop:waves.svg @center}Each colour travels as a wave. Red waves are long and lazy. Blue waves are short and quick.

When sunlight hits the air, it bumps into {pop:molecule.svg @left}tiny gas molecules.

Long red waves slide straight past them.

But short blue waves get bounced around in every direction. Scientists call this scattering.

So blue light reaches your eyes from every part of the sky.

And purple? The sun gives off less of it, and our eyes see it poorly.

At sunset, the light must cross much more air. The blue is scattered away, and the {pop:sunset.svg @right}reds and oranges are left.

So the sky is blue because small waves scatter the most.

Now you know. Look up, and tell a friend.
